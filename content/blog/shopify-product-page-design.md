A product page has a short window to earn a sale. The shopper decides within seconds whether the product is what they want, whether the store is trustworthy and how hard buying will be. Design shapes all three.

This guide covers the decisions that matter most on a Shopify product page, and why each one affects sales.

## 1. Make the product obvious

The main image, the product name and the price should be visible without scrolling on a phone. If the shopper has to search for what they're looking at, many will leave.

Use a large, clear main image, and let the shopper see alternative views easily. Show the product in use if you can, not only on a plain background.

## 2. Put the buying action where the eye goes

The add-to-cart button should be prominent, easy to tap and clearly labelled. Avoid multiple buttons competing for attention. If the product has options, such as size or colour, make them easy to choose, and show which option is selected.

## 3. Answer the next question early

Most shoppers have one or two questions before they buy: will it fit, will it work with my setup, how long does delivery take. Put the answers near the buying action, not at the bottom of a long page.

## 4. Build trust without clutter

Reviews, delivery information and returns policy all build trust. Show them clearly, in a consistent place, and keep the design calm. Too many badges and banners can make a store look less credible.

## 5. Use text that buyers can scan

Short paragraphs, clear headings and a bulleted list of key features help people read on a phone. Long blocks of text make the decision harder, not easier.

## 6. Design for the phone first

Most product pages are viewed on phones. Check that the image gallery, the options and the buying button all work with one thumb. Text should be large enough to read without zooming, and tap targets should be easy to hit.

## 7. Keep the page fast

Design choices affect speed. Large unoptimised images, heavy sliders and too many apps can slow a page enough to lose shoppers before they see the product. Compress images, limit apps and load the important content first.

## 8. Keep the page consistent with the brand

A product page should look like the rest of the store: the same colours, type, spacing and tone. Inconsistency makes a store feel less established, and that affects trust.

## 9. Test before you decide

Design decisions are easier to judge with evidence. Compare two versions of a product page, change one element at a time and measure the effect on adding to cart and on sales. Keep what works, and record why.

## Where to start

Choose your three best-selling products, look at each on a phone and ask whether the main image, price, options and buying button are clear within the first screen. Fix those first, then measure the result over a month.

Shopify makes it easy to launch a store, and it makes it easy to create SEO problems without noticing. Duplicate pages, generic titles and collections that don't say anything useful are common, and they build up quietly.

This checklist covers the Shopify settings and page types that matter most. Go through it in order, and fix the items on your highest-value pages first.

## 1. Check how Google sees your store

Open Google Search Console and look at the coverage report. Look for pages that are "discovered but not indexed," pages with errors and pages Google has chosen not to index. These show you where the store is leaking visibility.

## 2. Review your product titles

Shopify uses the product name as the default title, which is often too short or too vague. Rewrite titles so they name the product, state what it's for and include the main search term once. Keep them under about 60 characters.

## 3. Write unique meta descriptions

Each product and collection should have its own description. Shopify can generate them automatically, but the generated text is often thin. Write a short sentence that says what the buyer gets, and make sure no two pages share the same description.

## 4. Watch for duplicate product pages

Shopify lets the same product sit in several collections, and the URLs can create duplicate content. Use the canonical setting so the main product page is the one Google indexes. Check that variants don't create separate pages that compete with each other.

## 5. Structure your collections

Collection pages often rank for broader searches, so they should say what the collection is. Add a short introduction at the top, written for the buyer, with a clear heading. Don't rely only on a grid of products.

## 6. Fix your navigation and internal links

Link from guides and collections to the products they recommend, and back again. Internal links help Google understand which pages matter, and they help buyers move through the store. Check that the main menu links to your key collections and that none of them lead to error pages.

## 7. Check your filter and search pages

Filter combinations can create thousands of near-identical URLs. Decide which filter pages should be indexed, and block the rest. Ask a developer to check the robots settings and the canonical tags if you're not sure.

## 8. Clean up redirects and deleted products

When you delete a product, its page should redirect to the closest equivalent, not return an error. Check redirect chains too, where one redirect leads to another. Each extra step slows the page and wastes search signals.

## 9. Improve images and speed

Compress product images, give every image a descriptive alt text, and use the correct image sizes for mobile. Heavy images are one of the most common reasons a Shopify store is slow on a phone.

## 10. Add structured data

Shopify themes often include basic product markup, but it may be incomplete. Check that each product has its name, price, availability and reviews marked up correctly. Test a few pages with Google's rich results test.

## Where to start

Pick your top ten products and top five collections. Work through steps two, three and five on those pages first, then measure visits and sales for a month. The results show you whether the work is worth extending to the rest of the store.

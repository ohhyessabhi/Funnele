Most ecommerce owners choose an SEO agency the way they'd choose a plumber: they ask around, read a few reviews and pick the one that sounds confident. That works badly for SEO, because the results take months to show, and a weak partner can take a year of budget before anyone notices.

This guide gives you the questions to ask before you sign, and what a good answer sounds like.

## 1. Who will actually do the work?

Ask who runs your account day to day, and who you'll speak to when something goes wrong. A good agency names one accountable person and explains how the team supports them. A weak one shows you a senior name on the proposal and hands the work to people you never meet.

## 2. What will you check first?

A serious partner starts with the store itself: how Google reads it, how fast it loads, how the catalog is structured and what the search data says about your buyers. If the first answer is "we'll start writing content," ask why, before the site is understood.

## 3. How do you choose keywords?

Look for an answer that mentions buyer intent, competition and the real questions shoppers ask. Ask what the difficulty and volume figures are based on, and which tool they use. Be wary of any partner who promises a list of "guaranteed" keywords.

## 4. What does month one look like?

The first month is usually about fixes and setup, not rankings. Ask for a list of what they'll check and change in the first four weeks, and what they expect to move as a result.

## 5. How will you report progress?

Ask for a sample report. It should show visits and revenue from search, not only keyword positions. It should also explain what changed, why it matters, and what happens next. If the sample report is a chart with no explanation, you'll be guessing about your own business.

## 6. What do you need from my store?

Access to Search Console and analytics is normal. Ask what they'll change on your site, and how they handle approvals. A good partner tells you before any change goes live, and keeps a record of what was changed and when.

## 7. How do you handle links?

Link building is where many agencies cut corners. Ask where links come from, how they check the sites, and whether they remove harmful links that already point to your store. Avoid any partner who guarantees a number of links, or who can't name the kinds of sites they use.

## 8. What happens if we stop working together?

Ask what you keep if the contract ends: the reports, the documentation, the changes, and the access. You should own your site, your analytics and your content. You shouldn't need the agency to keep your store working.

## What a good answer sounds like

A good partner answers these questions with specifics. They name the people involved, explain what they'll check first, show a sample report, and say plainly what they can't promise. They also tell you when something won't work for your store, even if it means fewer sales for them.

Most store owners ask this question about three months in. By then they have spent money, seen a few changes, and still aren't seeing their products rank. It's a fair question, and the honest answer depends on where the store starts.

## The short answer

For most ecommerce stores, expect the first visible movement in six to twelve weeks, and meaningful revenue from search in three to six months. Stores with serious technical problems can take longer, because those problems have to be fixed before the content can work.

Anyone who promises rankings within a few weeks is describing a best case, or hoping you won't check their work.

## What happens in the first month

The first month is rarely about rankings. It's about making the store readable to search engines.

- Google crawls the site and finds which pages it can reach.
- Pages that are slow, duplicated or blocked get fixed.
- Page titles, descriptions and headings get rewritten, so each page has one clear job.

If you look at your rankings in month one, you'll mostly see the same results you had before. That's normal. The work is underneath, and it's the part that makes everything after it possible.

## What happens in months two and three

This is when movement usually starts to show. Pages that were fixed get crawled again, and product and collection pages begin to appear for the searches they were written for. If the store has buying guides, they start to attract readers who are still deciding what to buy.

Expect the numbers to move unevenly. A handful of keywords will climb first, usually the ones with less competition. Others will sit still for weeks, and that doesn't mean the work has failed.

## What changes the timeline

Four things decide how quickly a store moves.

1. **How many technical problems it has.** A store with broken links, duplicate collections and slow templates has to fix those first. Until then, new content has less to build on.
2. **How competitive its category is.** A search term that a large brand already dominates takes longer than a specific term nobody has targeted properly.
3. **How much useful content it publishes.** Articles that answer real questions build on each other over time. A single post rarely moves a store on its own.
4. **How trusted the domain is.** Links from relevant sites tell search engines the store is worth showing. Those links take time to earn, and they can't be bought quickly without risk.

## What a good plan looks like in the first 90 days

A good SEO partner should be able to show you, every month:

- What they fixed, and what each fix was expected to change.
- Which pages moved, and which didn't, with the reason where they know it.
- Visits and revenue from search, not only keyword positions.
- What they plan to do next, and why it comes next.

If a report only shows keyword positions with no revenue line and no explanation, ask harder questions. Rankings matter because they lead to visits and sales. Judge the work by the sales.

## Where to start

The first step is an honest look at the store. That covers the catalog, the margins, the searches buyers actually make, and the technical state of the site. From there, you should get a written order of work and a rough timeline before anything is changed on the live site.

If you want a specific answer for your store, that's what a store analysis is for.

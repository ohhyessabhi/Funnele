## PAGE: /problems/technical

**Heading:** What a technical SEO audit finds, and what we do about it

Technical problems are the quiet ones. A store can have good products, good copy and good links, and still lose visits because Google can't reach or read the right pages. These problems rarely show up in the store itself, so most owners don't know they exist.

**The common technical problems**

- **Pages Google can't reach.** Products that sit deep in the site, with no links pointing to them.
- **Pages that shouldn't be indexed.** Filter combinations and internal search results that add noise without adding value.
- **Redirect chains and broken links.** Each extra step slows the page and wastes the search signal.
- **Duplicate pages.** The same product in several collections, with several URLs competing with each other.
- **Slow templates.** A theme that loads heavy code on every page.

**How we find them**

We crawl the store the way a search engine does, and compare the results with Google Search Console. We look at which pages are indexed, which aren't, and why. We check speed on real devices, and we review the structured data.

**How we fix them**

We rank each problem by how much traffic and revenue it likely affects, and fix the most important ones first. Each fix is tested, and we check the results in the next crawl.

### Frequently asked questions

**Do we need developer access?** Often, yes. Some technical fixes need changes to the theme or site code. We'll tell you what's needed and who should make the change.

**How long does a technical audit take?** Most stores can be crawled and reviewed in one to two weeks. The fixes that follow depend on the size of the store.

## PAGE: /problems/content

**Heading:** What good ecommerce content looks like

Content helps a store rank for searches it can't win with product pages alone, and it helps buyers decide. The content that works answers a real question, in the words buyers use, and then links to the products that solve it.

**What we write**

- Buying guides that help a shopper choose between options.
- Answers to common questions about sizing, compatibility, care and use.
- Comparisons that are honest about trade-offs.
- Product and category pages with enough detail to answer the first questions a buyer has.

**How we decide what to write**

We start with the questions your customers already ask, from support emails, reviews and sales conversations. Then we check the search data to see how people phrase those questions. Each article has one main job and one clear next step.

**How we keep content useful**

Every article is written to a standard we'd be happy to show a buyer: specific, honest and easy to scan. Articles are reviewed before they go live, and we update them when the products or the questions change.

### Frequently asked questions

**How many articles do we need?** It depends on the store and its category. We agree a content plan based on the search demand and the products you want to grow.

**Who writes the articles?** We do. You review each one before it's published, and you can add your own knowledge where it helps.

## PAGE: /problems/on-page

**Heading:** How product and collection pages should read

Product and collection pages are where most sales happen, and they're often the weakest pages on a store. The titles are generic, the descriptions are missing and the copy is copied from the supplier. Search engines can't tell what the page is for, and neither can the buyer.

**What a strong product page includes**

- A title that names the product and states its use.
- A description that tells the buyer what they get.
- An opening paragraph that answers the most common question.
- Specifications in a clear list, with the details buyers search for.
- Images with descriptive alt text, and reviews where you have them.

**What a strong collection page includes**

A short introduction that says what the collection is for and who it suits. A clear heading. Links to the buying guides that help people choose. And a product grid that is easy to scan on a phone.

**How we work**

We start with the pages that sell the most, or should. We rewrite titles, descriptions and copy, check the result on phones and desktops, and track how each page performs.

### Frequently asked questions

**Will you rewrite all our products?** Not at once. We start with the most important pages and extend the work once the results are measured.

**Do you use the supplier's product copy?** We use it as a source of facts, but we rewrite it so the page isn't identical to every other store selling the same product.

## PAGE: /problems/links

**Heading:** How we earn links that help, and remove links that hurt

Links from other sites tell search engines that a store is worth trusting. The right links come from sites your buyers already read. The wrong links, from unrelated or low-quality sites, can hold a store back.

**What we do**

- **Audit.** We review every site that links to your store, and identify the ones that are unrelated, low quality or harmful.
- **Clean up.** Where harmful links are a real problem, we document them and submit a removal request to search engines.
- **Earn.** We find relevant publications, industry blogs and directories where your buyers spend time, and create content or expert contributions that give those sites a reason to link.

**What we won't do**

We don't buy links, use link networks or promise a number of links. Those methods can damage a store, and they don't last. Every link we build comes from a real relationship with a relevant site.

### Frequently asked questions

**How long do links take to show results?** Some take weeks to be published and indexed. Others take months to build trust. We report progress honestly, including the links that didn't happen.

**Do we need to approve each link?** Yes. You see every site we contact and every piece of content we propose before it goes out.

## PAGE: /problems/ai-search

**Heading:** How we help your store show up in AI answers

AI answer tools choose which sources to mention based on how clear, specific and trusted a store's information is. We can't promise a placement in any AI answer, and anyone who does is overselling. What we can do is make your store easier to find, understand and cite.

**What we do**

- **Check how AI tools answer today.** We ask the questions your buyers ask, and record which stores and sources come up.
- **Make product facts clear.** Specifications, sizing and compatibility stated plainly on the page, with structured data.
- **Build the sources.** Mentions and links from publications and directories that AI tools already rely on for your category.
- **Track it monthly.** We repeat the checks and report what changed, including what didn't.

**What makes AI answers different from search**

Search shows a list of results. An AI answer gives one response and names some of its sources. Being one of those sources depends on clarity, consistency and trust across the web, so the work overlaps heavily with good SEO.

### Frequently asked questions

**Do you use special tools for AI search?** We use a mix of testing, search data and our own checks. The field is new, so we're open about what we can and can't measure.

**Is AEO or GEO a separate service?** For most stores, it's part of the SEO plan. We track it alongside rankings and revenue.

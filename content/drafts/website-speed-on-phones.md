Most ecommerce traffic now arrives on phones, often on a mobile connection that is slower than the office Wi-Fi a developer tests on. A page that feels fine on a laptop can take long enough on a phone for the shopper to give up before the product appears.

This guide explains where the time goes, how to measure it and what to fix first.

## Why phones are different

Phones have slower processors and less memory than laptops, and they often load pages over less reliable connections. Scripts that run quickly on a desktop can take a noticeable time to run on a phone. Test the store on a real phone, or on a mobile test profile, not only on your desktop.

## Measure what the shopper experiences

A single speed score doesn't tell you much. Look at how quickly the main content appears, how soon the page responds when someone taps, and whether the layout jumps around while loading. These are the things a shopper notices.

Google's PageSpeed Insights and the Chrome User Experience Report both show real-world data for your pages. Use them to find which pages are slowest, and check the results for the most important product and collection pages.

## The usual causes

Most slow ecommerce pages suffer from the same few problems:

- **Large images.** Product photos uploaded at full size and shown much smaller. Compress them, and serve the sizes each screen needs.
- **Too many scripts.** Tracking, chat, reviews and promotional tools, each adding its own code to every page.
- **Apps that load everywhere.** A theme app or plugin that runs on every page, even where it isn't used.
- **Heavy fonts and animations.** Several font families, or motion effects that block the page while it loads.
- **Slow hosting or unoptimised code.** Less common, but it can add a delay before anything loads.

## The order to fix things in

Fix the heaviest problems first. In most stores, that means images, then scripts, then apps.

1. **Images.** Resize and compress the largest product photos, and make sure images below the fold load only when needed.
2. **Scripts and apps.** Remove apps you don't use. Load the rest after the main content has appeared, where that's possible.
3. **Fonts and layout.** Limit the number of font weights, and reserve space for images so the page doesn't jump while it loads.
4. **Hosting and code.** If the basics are fixed and the page is still slow, look at the server response and the theme code.

## Check the result on real devices

After each change, test again on a phone. Look at the same pages and compare the results. A fix is only useful if the page feels faster to the shopper.

## Keep it fast after launch

Speed slips over time. Every new app, banner and tracking tag adds weight. Review the store's speed every month, and check any new app before it's installed. Agree a simple rule with your team: nothing goes live that makes the main product pages slower.

## Where to start

Choose your five most visited product pages. Test each on a phone, fix the largest images and remove any apps you don't use. Measure the speed and the add-to-cart rate for a month, and use those results to decide the next step.
